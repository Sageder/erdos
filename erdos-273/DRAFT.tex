\title{Structural constraints on covering systems with all moduli of the form $p-1$,\\
$p\ge5$ prime\\[2mm]
\large (Erd\H{o}s Problem 273: partial results; the problem is \emph{not} resolved here)}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Erd\H{o}s and Graham asked whether there is a covering system of $\mathbb Z$ all of whose moduli
are of the form $p-1$ for primes $p\ge5$. We do \emph{not} answer this. We record a set of
rigorously proved structural constraints and reductions, each independently audited and
machine-verified, together with an explicit determination of why several natural attacks cannot
work. In particular we prove: an exact parity-split equivalence reducing the problem to the
existence of two \emph{disjoint} covering systems inside the halved set $H=\{m:2m+1\text{ prime}\}$;
that the reciprocal-sum (``budget'') obstruction is powerless in both directions, since the
infimum of $\sum 1/n_i$ over distinct-moduli covering systems is exactly $1$; a rigidity theorem
identifying the dyadic staircase as the unique exact cover of $\mathbb Z$ minus one class by
distinct moduli, whence the only known cost-minimising mechanism is unavailable in this setting;
and two effective elimination tests (a coprimality/pigeonhole test and an exact $q$-adic fiber
test) which together rule out explicit ranges of possible least common multiples. All claims are
accompanied by independent verification scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

A \emph{covering system} is a finite list of residue classes $a_i \pmod{n_i}$, $1\le i\le k$, with
$1<n_1<n_2<\cdots<n_k$ (so the moduli are pairwise distinct and exceed $1$) and
$\bigcup_i (a_i+n_i\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$. Put
\[
E:=\{p-1:\ p\ \text{prime},\ p\ge5\}=\{n\ge4:\ n+1\ \text{prime}\},\qquad
H:=\{m\ge2:\ 2m+1\ \text{prime}\}=\tfrac12E .
\]
Every $n\in E$ is even, and $n\mapsto n/2$ is a bijection $E\to H$. We write
$B_E(X)=\sum_{n\in E,\ n\le X}1/n$ and, for $L\ge1$, $f(L)=\sum_{n\mid L,\ n\in E}1/n$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Question (Erd\H{o}s--Graham, 1980).} Is there a covering system with all moduli in
$E$?

\section{The parity split}

\begin{lemma}[Parity split]\label{lem:parity}
The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] There is a covering system with all moduli in $E$.
\item[\rm(ii)] There are two \emph{disjoint} finite sets $M_0,M_1\subseteq H$ such that for each
$j$ there is a covering system of $\mathbb Z$ whose set of moduli is exactly $M_j$.
\end{enumerate}
Moreover $\operatorname{lcm}(E\text{-moduli})=2\operatorname{lcm}(H\text{-moduli})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii). Let $\{a_i\bmod n_i\}$ be such a system, $n_i=2m_i$ with $m_i\in H$ pairwise
distinct. Since $n_i$ is even, $a_i+n_i\mathbb Z$ consists solely of integers of the parity of
$a_i$; so $I_j=\{i:a_i\equiv j\ (2)\}$ partitions the index set, and $M_j=\{m_i:i\in I_j\}$ are
disjoint. Fix $j$ and $y\in\mathbb Z$; put $x=2y+j$. Some $i$ has $x\equiv a_i\ (2m_i)$, forcing
$a_i\equiv j\ (2)$, i.e. $i\in I_j$; writing $a_i=2b_i+j$,
\[
2y+j\equiv 2b_i+j \pmod{2m_i}\iff 2(y-b_i)\in 2m_i\mathbb Z\iff y\equiv b_i\pmod{m_i},
\]
so $\{b_i\bmod m_i\}_{i\in I_j}$ covers $\mathbb Z$. (The argument quantifies over all
$y\in\mathbb Z$, negative ones included; classes are two-sided.)

(ii)$\Rightarrow$(i). Given coverings $\{b^{(j)}_m\bmod m\}_{m\in M_j}$, take the classes
$2b^{(j)}_m+j \pmod{2m}$ for all $m\in M_j$, $j=0,1$. The moduli $2m$ lie in $E$ and are pairwise
distinct (within a block because the $m$ are, across blocks because $M_0\cap M_1=\varnothing$).
If $x=2y+j$ then $y\equiv b^{(j)}_m\ (m)$ for some $m\in M_j$, whence
$x\equiv 2b^{(j)}_m+j\ (2m)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:pivot}
Since $2,3\in H$ each occur once, at most one of $M_0,M_1$ contains $2$ and at most one contains
$3$. Hence an affirmative answer requires, in particular, a covering system of $\mathbb Z$ with
distinct moduli all of the form $(p-1)/2$, $p\ge7$ prime, i.e.\ an $H$-covering avoiding the
modulus $2$. Contrapositively: if no such system exists, the answer to the problem is negative.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
Any construction forcing one of $M_0,M_1$ to consist of odd moduli only is a dead end: that is the
Erd\H{o}s--Selfridge odd covering problem, which is open.
\end{remark}

\section{The budget obstruction is powerless}

\begin{lemma}[Doubling map]\label{lem:double}
If $C=\{a_i\bmod n_i\}$ is a covering system with distinct moduli, so is
$D(C)=\{0\bmod2\}\cup\{(2a_i+1)\bmod 2n_i\}$, and
$\operatorname{cost}(D(C))=\tfrac12+\tfrac12\operatorname{cost}(C)$, where
$\operatorname{cost}=\sum_i 1/n_i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Even $x$ lie in $0\bmod 2$. If $x=2y+1$, pick $i$ with $y\equiv a_i\ (n_i)$; then
$x\equiv 2a_i+1\ (2n_i)$. The moduli are $2$ and the pairwise distinct $2n_i\ge4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:inf}
$\inf\sum_i 1/n_i=1$ over covering systems with distinct moduli, and the infimum is not attained.
Explicitly, iterating Lemma~\ref{lem:double} from $C_0=\{0(2),0(3),1(4),5(6),7(12)\}$ gives systems
$C_k$ with $\sum 1/n_i=1+\tfrac13 2^{-k}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Subadditivity of density gives $\sum 1/n_i\ge1$; equality would make the system an exact cover
with distinct moduli (two classes are disjoint or meet in a class of positive density), which is
impossible by Davenport--Mirsky--Newman--Rado. The upper bound is Lemma~\ref{lem:double}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Consequently no reciprocal-sum argument can decide the problem in either direction: costs may be
arbitrarily close to $1$, and $\sum_{p\ge5}1/(p-1)$ diverges. (Filaseta and Kalogirou,
arXiv:2407.15280, prove the sharper statement that a positive lower bound on the excess holds
precisely when the least modulus exceeds $4$; since $4\in E$, our setting lies in the regime where
no such bound is available.) Lemma~\ref{lem:double} does not act on $E$: admissibility of $n$ and
of $2n$ are unrelated conditions.
\end{remark}

\section{Rigidity of the cheap mechanism}

\begin{theorem}[Rigidity]\label{thm:rigid}
If residue classes with pairwise distinct moduli $>1$ partition $\mathbb Z\setminus(c\bmod D)$
exactly, then $D=2^m$ and the moduli are exactly $2,4,\dots,2^m$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From $\sum_i z^{a_i}/(1-z^{n_i})=\frac1{1-z}-\frac{z^{c}}{1-z^{D}}$, evaluate at a primitive
$n_k$-th root of unity, $n_k$ the largest modulus: only $i=k$ contributes a pole, forcing $n_k=D$,
$D$ even and $a_k\equiv c+D/2$. Deleting that class leaves an exact partition of
$\mathbb Z\setminus(c\bmod D/2)$; induct.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
$E\cap\{2^k\}=\{4,16,256,65536\}$ and $H\cap\{2^k\}=\{2,8,128,32768\}$ (Fermat primes minus one,
respectively halved). Neither contains two consecutive powers of $2$, and $2\notin E$. Hence there
is no exact partition of $\mathbb Z$ minus one class by distinct $E$-moduli at all, and only the
trivial one ($D=2$) by distinct $H$-moduli. The unique known mechanism driving the cost to $1$ is
therefore unavailable here.
\end{corollary}

\section{Two effective elimination tests}

\begin{lemma}[Forced overlap]\label{lem:A2}
Let $M$ be the modulus set of a covering system with distinct moduli and let $T\subseteq M$ be
pairwise coprime. Then
\[
\sum_{m\in M}\frac1m-1\ \ge\ f(T):=\sum_{m\in T}\frac1m-1+\prod_{m\in T}\Bigl(1-\frac1m\Bigr).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a family $S$ of chosen classes put $g(S)=\sum_{m\in S}1/m-\operatorname{dens}(\bigcup S)$.
Adding a class of modulus $m'$ raises the first term by $1/m'$ and the density by at most $1/m'$,
so $g$ is non-decreasing. $g(M)=\sum_M 1/m-1$ since the union is $\mathbb Z$. For pairwise coprime
$T$ the classes are independent by the Chinese remainder theorem, \emph{whatever the residues},
so $\operatorname{dens}(\bigcup T)=1-\prod_{m\in T}(1-1/m)$ exactly, i.e. $g(T)=f(T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:A3}
If $60\mid L$ and $1<f(L)\le\frac{31}{30}$, then no covering system with all moduli in $E$ has
lcm dividing $L$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$60\mid L$ gives $4,6,10\mid L$, all in $E$. Since $f(L)-1\le\frac1{30}<\frac1{10}$, each of
$4,6,10$ is forced: deleting it leaves reciprocal sum $<1$. Their halves $2,3,5$ are pairwise
coprime, and with $X_j=\sum_{m\in M_j}1/m-1$ we have $X_0+X_1\le 2f(L)-2\le\frac1{15}$ while both
$X_j>0$ strictly (Theorem~\ref{thm:inf}); so $X_j<\frac1{15}$ for each $j$. As
$f(\{2,3\})=\frac16$, $f(\{2,5\})=\frac1{10}$, $f(\{3,5\})=\frac1{15}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:A2} forbids
any two of $2,3,5$ from lying in the same $M_j$. Three objects cannot occupy two blocks.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$q$-adic fiber test]\label{lem:fiber}
Let $q$ be a prime, $S$ a set of $H$-moduli, and for an assignment of residues $b_m$ and
$r\in\mathbb Z_q$ put $F_q(r)=\sum_{m:\,b_m\equiv r\ (q^{\nu_q(m)})} q^{\nu_q(m)}/m$. If some
covering system with all moduli in $E$ has $\operatorname{lcm}/2=L_H$ and
$S=\{m\mid L_H:2m+1\text{ prime}\}$, then $\Phi_q(S):=\max_{\text{assignments}}\min_r F_q(r)\ge2$
for every prime $q\mid L_H$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a single covering system, the integers $y\equiv r\ (q^J)$ that the class $b_m\ (m)$ can meet
are exactly those with $b_m\equiv r\ (q^{\nu_q(m)})$, inside which the class has relative density
$q^{\nu_q(m)}/m$; subadditivity forces $F_q(r)\ge1$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:parity} the two halves are
disjoint subsets of $S$ carrying their own residues, so their fiber functions add. Enlarging the
used set only increases $F_q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The Haar average of $F_q$ over $r$ equals $\sum 1/n_i$ exactly, so $\sum 1/n_i>1$ is only the
\emph{average} condition, whereas covering demands the \emph{minimum}.
\end{remark}

\section{What is ruled out, and what is not}

Combining $f(L)>1$, Theorem~\ref{thm:A3} and Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} (the last computed exactly by
branch and bound) eliminates explicit ranges of candidate least common multiples; the computations
and their independent verifications are in the accompanying scripts. The smallest $L$ with
$f(L)>1$ is $55440=2^4\cdot3^2\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11$, and $55440$ is itself eliminated, twice
independently (by Lemma~\ref{lem:fiber} at $q=2$ and by the strengthened form of
Theorem~\ref{thm:A3}).

Finally we record an obstruction to the obstructions. For every fixed finite set $Q$ of primes,
any $m\in S$ coprime to $\prod Q$ contributes $1/m$ to every $Q$-cell for every residue
assignment, and $\sum_{m\in H,\ \gcd(m,\prod Q)=1}1/m=\infty$ by Mertens--Dirichlet. Hence no
condition local at a fixed finite set of primes can prove a negative answer; explicit finite
certificates confirm this ($H\cap[2,82899]$ already satisfies every single-prime fiber condition
with threshold $2$). A negative resolution therefore requires a functional coupling all primes
with a bound uniform in their number --- that is the distortion technology of Hough and of
Balister--Bollob\'as--Morris--Sahasrabudhe--Tiba, which provably requires least modulus at least
$616000$, whereas $\min E=4$.

\section*{Status}

The problem is \textbf{not resolved} by this note. The results above are constraints and
reductions only. The decisive open question is Corollary~\ref{cor:pivot}: whether $\mathbb Z$
admits a covering system with distinct moduli all in $H\setminus\{2\}$.
