                                                   Abstract. We consider several old problems involving the number of prime divisors function
                                                   ω(n), as well as the related functions Ω(n) and τ (n). Firstly, we show that there are infinitely
                                                   many positive integers n such that ω(n + k) ≤ Ω(n + k) ≪ k for all positive
                                                                                                                             P integers nk, establishing
                                                   a conjecture of Erdős and Straus. Secondly, we show that the series ∞
arXiv:2512.01739v2 [math.NT] 25 Apr 2026




                                                                                                                               n=1 ω(n)/2 is irrational,
                                                   settling a conjecture of Erdős. Thirdly, we prove an asymptotic formula conjectured by Erdős,
                                                   Pomerance and Sárközy for the number of n ≤ x satisfying ω(n) = ω(n + 1), for almost all x,
                                                   with similar results for Ω and τ . Common to the resolution of all these problems is the use of
                                                   the probabilistic method. For the first problem, this is combined with computations involving a
                                                   high-dimensional sieve of Maynard-type. For the second and third problems, we instead make use
                                                   of a general quantitative estimate for two-point correlations of multiplicative functions with a small
                                                   power of logarithm saving that may be of independent interest. This correlation estimate is derived
                                                   by using recent work of Pilatte.

1.2. A sieve-theoretic result. Our first result, proven in Section 2, settles a conjecture of Erdős
and Straus [13], [2, Problem #248], also stated by Erdős and Graham [18, p.61]:
Theorem 1.1 (Erdős #248). There exists an absolute constant C > 0 such that for infinitely many
positive integers n, for all positive integers k we have
(1.6)                                      ω(n + k) ≤ Ω(n + k) ≤ Ck.
In particular, by (1.5) we have τ (n + k) ≤ 2Ck for such n for all positive integers k.
   If k was restricted to a bounded range then this claim would easily follow from the fundamental
lemma of sieve theory; the difficulty thus lies in the unbounded nature of k. Indeed, in [18, p. 61],
Erdős and Graham write “We just know too little about sieves to handle such a question (“we” here
means not just us but the collective wisdom (?) of our poor struggling human race)”. However, it
turns out that a variant of the Maynard sieve [40], which was introduced several decades after that
paper, can establish this result (via an approach similar to that in [44], [45]).
   The strategy of proof can be easily described as follows. For simplicity we begin with the task
of showing (1.6) for ω and some n in a large interval [x, 2x]. In view of the Hardy–Ramanujan
law that ω(n) ≈ log2 x for most n ∈ [x, 2x], the main difficulty is to establish (1.6) in the range
k ≤ C10 log2 x for a large constant C0 . Here we perform a Maynard-type sieve (in fact, a product of
smooth one-dimensional Selberg sieves) on [x, 2x], sieving n + k to mostly avoid being divisible by
                         k
primes less than x1/100 for 1 ≤ k ≤ C10 log2 x. As is now well known, one can compute bounded
order correlations (e.g., pair correlations or fourth order correlations) of events such as p | n + k for
n sampled using this sieve to quite high accuracy for all primes less than, say, x1/100 , and one can
then establish (1.6) with positive probability on this sieve by standard applications of the moment
method (we will rely primarily on the second and fourth moments). The main technical issue is
that the dimension ⌊ C10 log2 x⌋ of the sieve is growing slowly with x, in contrast with most other
applications of Maynard-type sieves in which the sieve dimension is bounded; but the dimension
growth turns out to be sufficiently slow that a suitably careful computation of main terms and error
terms still lets one close the argument. To pass from ω to Ω, one needs to account for divisibility
by squares and higher powers of p, but the fact that the sieve can be built out of divisibility criteria
with respect to squarefree moduli makes these additional terms relatively easy to handle.
Remark 1.2. In [14] (see also [2, Problem #679]), the variant question was posed whether, for any
ε > 0, there existed infinitely many n such that ω(n − k) < (1 + ε) log k/ log2 k for all k < n that
are sufficiently large depending on ε. This appears to be beyond the capability of our methods to
show, as is the variant in [15], [14], [2, Problem #647] asking whether there is any n > 24 such that
τ (n − k) ≤ k + 2 for all 1 ≤ k ≤ n.
   In [15] (see also [2, Problem #413], [14], [18], [29, Problem B8], and OEIS A005236), it was asked
whether there are infinitely many n which were barriers for ω in the sense that ω(n − k) ≤ k for
all 1 ≤ k ≤ n. It may be that the methods of this paper can be pushed (particularly if one is more
careful about how bounds depend on the sieve dimension) to establish2 a result of this form for
sufficiently large k; however, to handle small values of k, such as k = 1, 2, it seems that the problem
is of comparable difficulty to the prime tuples conjecture (or the Sophie Germain conjecture that
there are infinitely many primes p with 2p + 1 also prime), and we make no progress on it here.
   2In fact, after the release of this preprint, bounds of the form ω(n ± k) ≤ Ω(n ± k) ≪ log k were established for
all 2 ≤ k ≤ n, by refining the methods of this paper; see [36].
